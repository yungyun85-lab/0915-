01
BACKGROUND & PROBLEM DEFINITION

Why & Problem

OBSERVATION & PERSONAL TRIGGER

From Banff National Park to Multi-Wall Space

  • While working as a student assistant in CYCU's immersive venue, I saw how extending visuals across multiple walls creates spatial enclosure that a single flat screen cannot provide.
  • During a trip to Lake Louise in Banff National Park, witnessing majestic mountain scenery followed later by wildfire sirens and news reports caused a sharp shift from awe to anxiety.
  • That contrast led to the central theme: "the nature in your memory may no longer exist."
CORE PROBLEM

Psychological Distance & Single-Screen Limits

  • Climate change and ecological destruction involve long timescales and cross-regional impacts, so viewers often perceive them as distant events rather than urgent daily realities.
  • Text and data alone rarely trigger emotional connection; the project aimed to reduce psychological distance through spatial imagery and sound.
  • Traditional single rectangular screens restrict viewing to one front-facing frame, limiting peripheral immersion.
02
SPATIAL CONFIGURATION & TIMELINE

Spatial & Narrative Design

MODE A · VISUAL ENCLOSURE

Three-Wall Panorama

Pristine natural landscapes extend continuously across the left, center, and right walls, wrapping viewers inside a unified visual environment.

MODE B · SPATIAL GAZE GUIDANCE

Asymmetric Contrast

Differentiated clips play simultaneously across the three walls—contrasting pre-disaster nature and post-disaster ruins—to prompt viewers to scan across the space.

SHOT AUDIO PACE THREE-WALL VISUAL ARRANGEMENT
01 Build-up · Rising Tension

Unified three-wall panorama with slow transitions synced to the music to establish calm natural atmosphere.

02 Climax · Peak Energy

Asymmetric stepped multi-grid switching on the beat (Center: 12 slices, Left: 8 slices, Right: 4 slices) to raise visual density.

03 Release · Driving Rhythm

All three walls play identical visuals synchronously, merging back into a single unified panorama.

04 Acceleration · High Urgency

Fast cuts on heavy beats: Left wall shows intact forests, oceans, and cities; Center shows a rotating Earth timeline; Right wall shows dead trees, desert sand, and ruins.

05 Transition · Deceleration

Visuals slow down, overexpose, or fade to black as volume drops, shifting intensity into quiet mourning.

06 Resolution · Lingering Resonance

On-screen text fades in and out sentence by sentence across black space and acoustic silence, leaving room for reflection.

03
TECHNICAL CHALLENGES & HUMAN-AI WORKFLOW

Production & Execution

MULTI-SCREEN EDITING CapCut + Split Script + Arena

Solving Ultra-Wide Distortion Across Three Walls

  • Challenge: Stretching a single video asset across three projection walls caused severe pixelation and distortion.
  • Solution: Multiplied single-wall resolution (1890 × 1080) by three to build an ultra-wide 5670 × 1080, 60 fps timeline in CapCut.
  • Stitched multiple assets side-by-side with edge feathering and color adjustment, processed the export through a lab assistant's splitting script, and synchronized playback via Arena software.
AI COLLABORATION Human-in-the-Loop

Maintaining Continuity in AI-Generated Restoration Clips

  • Challenge: AI-generated clips can break spatial continuity or diverge from the narrative pace if generated without strict constraints.
  • Solution: Used Gemini Pro for prompt iteration and generated three specific restoration segments—water purification, vegetation regrowth on scorched earth, and urban smog dissipation.
  • Tuned prompts with constraints such as "keep composition static, only transition water from murky to clear," while handling all story structure, asset selection, editing, and audio mixing independently.

DESIGN REFLECTION

Limitations & Next Steps

01 · IMMERSION VS. INTERACTIVITY

Without gesture sensing or real-time viewer input, this piece functions as a pre-sequenced spatial narrative rather than an interactive system.

02 · SCOPE & PACING CONSTRAINTS

Production time limits prevented finishing the planned floor projection, and the mid-video shift from crisis to technological recovery was relatively abrupt.

03 · LACK OF AUDIENCE DATA

Because I am no longer working as a student assistant at the venue, I could not re-exhibit the work or run questionnaires and interviews to test psychological distance.

04 · FUTURE RESEARCH DIRECTION

Future iterations will introduce behavior-responsive sensing, smoother narrative transitions, and structured studies on viewer attention allocation across multiple walls.
